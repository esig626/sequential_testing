# Xing 2025 and controlled sequential testing literature audit

Date: 16 September 2026.

Status: literature synthesis. The paper by Yiming Xing and its supplement were read directly. Related papers were checked through primary publication pages, arXiv records, or publisher abstracts as noted below. This note is intended to set the novelty boundary and identify reusable methods for `sequential_testing`; it is not itself a theorem.

## 2. What the Xing paper actually studies

There are `K` independent data streams. Within each stream the observations may be temporally dependent. Stream `k` has `M` composite hypotheses and an indifference zone. A sequential procedure has one common stopping time and, at stopping, returns one hypothesis label for every stream.

The paper therefore combines:

1. multiple simultaneous testing problems;
2. multiple composite hypotheses in each stream;
3. temporal dependence within a stream;
4. a common sequential stopping rule;
5. a global error criterion controlling the probability of at least `s` misclassifications at several user chosen levels;
6. asymptotic minimisation of expected sample size as the requested error levels go to zero.

This is not the same model as the present repository. In Xing's paper, the reported decision does not determine the law of future observations. All streams continue to be sampled under their own fixed data generating mechanisms until one terminal decision vector is produced. In our model, an edge decision is itself an action that selects the next node and can change the conditional law of future observations.

The distinction is essential: Xing provides a strong theory for passive dependent sequential data, not for controlled future experiments generated by earlier reported decisions.

## 3. The most important idea to import: general information functions

The paper does not insist that information grows as `n` times a constant divergence. For a parameter `theta` outside a wrong composite class, it introduces increasing information functions

`I_k^m(n; theta)`

and their inverse information times. In the iid case these reduce to the minimum cumulative Kullback Leibler divergence from the true parameter to the wrong composite class. In the general theory they are only required to satisfy regular growth properties and likelihood comparison assumptions.

This is extremely relevant to our problem. Once the future experiment depends on history and actions, there is no reason to expect the useful information scale to be exactly linear in stage count. The correct asymptotic variable may be an accumulated information clock generated by the realised controlled experiment.

The supplement makes this concrete. For independent Gaussian observations with unequal known variances, the information clock is

`psi(n) = sum_{t <= n} 1 / sigma(t)^2`,

so the information to a wrong interval is `psi(n)` times the appropriate squared parameter distance. The clock may be nonlinear in `n`. For AR(1) and finite Markov examples the information is again obtained from the long run growth of log likelihood ratios, with dependence handled through ergodic and complete convergence arguments.

### Import for this repository

A later asymptotic theory should not start by assuming a constant per stage divergence. It should first define a controlled information process or controlled information function under a fixed policy and environment, and only specialise to linear growth when justified.

For example, a future object might have the schematic form

`I_t^wrong(phi, eta)`

or, along a realised history,

`I_t^wrong(H_{t-1}; phi, eta)`,

where the definition must respect the distinction between rectangular and coupled environments. This is only a research direction, not a definition adopted by the manuscript yet.

## 4. Separation between validity and optimality assumptions

A particularly useful structural lesson is that Xing separates the assumptions needed for a valid test from those needed for asymptotic optimality.

The paper's Assumption 2 requires the adaptive log likelihood to separate from every wrong composite hypothesis almost surely. Under this condition alone, the proposed procedure terminates almost surely and admits the stated exponential error control.

The stronger Assumptions 1 and 3 control the asymptotic growth rate of likelihood evidence. They are used to obtain the lower and upper expected sample size bounds and hence first order asymptotic optimality.

### Import for this repository

We should follow the same hierarchy.

First layer: finite sample or nonasymptotic validity of local or pathwise error control under weak conditional moment, martingale, or likelihood assumptions.

Second layer: information growth assumptions giving sample complexity or stopping time bounds.

Third layer: matching lower bounds and asymptotic optimality.

This avoids putting ergodicity, complete convergence, or linear information growth into a theorem whose only purpose is error validity.

## 5. Xing's global error metric

Let the terminal decision vector be compared with the true vector of stream labels. The paper controls

`P_theta(number of misclassifications >= s)`

for every `s`, with potentially different tolerance levels `alpha_s`.

The metric includes ordinary probability of any misclassification and the generalised probability of at least `s` mistakes as special cases.

This is conceptually useful because our global path risk is also more informative than a single local Type I or Type II error. However, the metric cannot be imported mechanically. Xing counts simultaneous coordinate errors at one terminal time, whereas our event is the occurrence of an erroneous edge somewhere along a selected path. Earlier errors can also alter which later statistical problems are encountered.

A useful future extension may consider graded path losses such as the probability of at least `s` wrong edge decisions, but the current primary global object remains the probability of any wrong edge.

## 6. The asymptotic lower bound and its information geometry

For each stream, the relevant local asymptotic difficulty is governed by the second closest hypothesis in information distance. For a target of at least `s` misclassifications, the paper sums the `s` smallest such information functions across streams. Its lower bound is the inverse time at which this total information reaches approximately `|log alpha|`.

The proposed procedure then uses local adaptive evidence statistics, sorts them, and stops once every required sum of the smallest evidence values crosses its threshold. The upper bound matches the lower bound to first order.

### Import for this repository

The exact order statistic construction is specific to simultaneous independent streams. The broader principle is reusable:

* lower bounds identify the least informative collection of mistakes that can realise the error event;
* the sample complexity is governed by the information needed to rule out that least separated alternative;
* the threshold scale is logarithmic in the target error probability.

For our tree, the analogue should involve the least informative wrong path or wrong environment consistent with an erroneous edge event. Because the path is controlled and only one branch is realised, this optimisation will not be the sum of independent stream information functions.

## 7. Universal thresholds versus calibrated thresholds

Xing proves the finite threshold guarantee

`alpha_s <= binom(K,s) (M-1)^s exp(-a_s)`

and therefore obtains a universal threshold by adding the logarithm of the combinatorial factor to `|log alpha_s|`.

The paper explicitly notes that these universal thresholds can be conservative and recommends offline importance sampling to calibrate sharper thresholds.

### Import for this repository

There are two separate lessons.

First, conservative pathwise error guarantees may be obtained by a counting or union argument once the correct path event structure is identified. We must not multiply local correctness probabilities, and the selected path dependence must remain explicit.

Second, sharp finite sample calibration of rare path errors is likely to require rare event simulation. This fits directly with the rare action obstruction already recorded in F001.

## 8. Importance sampling for rare misclassification events

The paper uses Wald's likelihood ratio identity to estimate extremely small misclassification probabilities. The change of measure moves a selected subset of streams towards the closest parameters in the wrong hypotheses, making the rare error event common under the simulation law while retaining an exact likelihood correction.

This is highly relevant to the present repository. The analogue would be to tilt towards a closest wrong path, wrong local hypothesis, or adverse environment, while retaining the same policy and the correct controlled history dynamics.

The main warning is that our change of measure cannot be chosen independently at every history unless the uncertainty class is rectangular. For coupled environments, the rare event proposal law must itself be a coherent path law.

## 9. What the supplement teaches about dependence

The supplement verifies the assumptions in three examples:

* independent Gaussian observations with unequal variances;
* AR(1) observations;
* finite state Markov chains.

The examples show that temporal dependence is compatible with sequential composite testing theory when the log likelihood process has the required strong or complete convergence properties. In the AR(1) and Markov cases the proof strategy is essentially to identify a predictable long run likelihood drift and control the fluctuations around it.

This is useful for model classes we may later use as controlled examples. It does **not** settle our controlled case, because under our policy the transition mechanism itself can change after an action.

The supplement's proof decomposition, separating predictable information growth from stochastic fluctuation terms, is a useful template for checking any future controlled information assumption.

## 10. Literature boundary after the deeper search

The deep search changes the novelty boundary of this repository in an important way.

### 10.1 Action dependent future observations are established literature

Chernoff's sequential design of experiments is the classical starting point. Controlled sensing and active sequential hypothesis testing explicitly allow a decision maker to choose an experiment or control that changes the law of the next observation.

Therefore the statement

> sequential hypothesis testing in which actions determine future observation laws

is **not** a novelty claim available to us.

### 10.2 Controlled sensing with multiple hypotheses is established

Nitinawarat, Atia and Veeravalli study controlled sensing for multihypothesis testing in fixed sample and sequential settings. Adaptive causal sensing can outperform open loop sensing in multiple hypothesis problems, and asymptotically optimal sequential schemes are known.

Naghshvar and Javidi study active sequential hypothesis testing from a Bayesian and dynamic programming perspective, with information state methods and asymptotic performance bounds.

### 10.3 Controlled Markov observations are established

Nitinawarat and Veeravalli study sequential multihypothesis testing with controlled Markovian observations and nonuniform control costs. Their model has memory in the controlled observation process, an asymptotically optimal self tuning control rule, and a second procedure that meets separate decision risk constraints nonasymptotically while retaining asymptotic optimality.

Thus neither temporal dependence nor controlled Markov dependence is novel by itself.

### 10.4 Composite hypotheses plus controlled sensing are established

Deshmukh, Veeravalli and Bhashyam study sequential controlled sensing for composite multihypothesis testing in single parameter exponential families. Their policy controls the error probability and asymptotically attains an information theoretic lower bound on expected delay.

Therefore the conjunction

> composite hypotheses + sequential testing + action controlled observations

is also **not** a novelty claim available to us.

### 10.5 Dependent multiple streams and non iid sequential testing are established

Song and Fellouris establish asymptotically optimal sequential multiple testing under generalised error control when independent streams have local likelihood ratios satisfying strong laws.

Xing and Fellouris develop multistage testing for non iid data.

Chaudhuri and Fellouris allow dependence across data streams in joint sequential detection and isolation and obtain first order optimal expected sample sizes under several error criteria.

Hence generic temporal dependence, non iid sampling, and even dependence across streams are well developed asymptotically.

### 10.6 Finite sample active hypothesis testing is emerging

Kartik, Nayyar and Mitra give fixed horizon active hypothesis testing formulations with finite horizon lower and upper bounds that become asymptotically tight.

Lin, Nguyen, Xu and Ruchkin (2026) give a nonasymptotic expected stopping time upper bound for an elimination based active hypothesis testing procedure.

This means that finite sample analysis is not absent from active testing, although the specific finite sample composite, controlled, pathwise Rényi problem in this repository was not located in this search.

### 10.7 Rényi divergence for dependent Markov models is established

Fuh, Fuh, Liu and Wang study Rényi divergence rates for general hidden Markov models using a Markov representation and eigenvalues of an associated operator. This is relevant to long horizon Rényi calculations for fixed dependent models, but it does not provide the present controlled composite testing theory.

## 11. The closest existing papers to our model

The closest neighbours found in this search are:

1. **Nitinawarat and Veeravalli (2015)** for causal control with Markov memory and nonasymptotic risk constraints.
2. **Deshmukh, Veeravalli and Bhashyam (2021)** for composite hypotheses with controlled sensing.
3. **Naghshvar and Javidi (2013)** for dynamic programming and policy design in active sequential hypothesis testing.
4. **Xing (SS-2025-0042)** for composite sequential testing with general nonlinear information clocks and temporal dependence.
5. **Chaudhuri and Fellouris (2024)** for dependent sequential multiple testing.
6. **Fuh et al. (2025)** for Rényi divergence rates in Markov and hidden Markov models.

None of these sources, as checked in this audit, directly combines all of the following features:

* a tree of successive local composite binary testing problems;
* each reported local decision is simultaneously a statistical conclusion and the action choosing the next experiment;
* future composite classes may depend on the realised observation and decision history;
* a node based truth map remains fixed across histories reaching the same node;
* global loss is pathwise, such as the probability of any wrong edge;
* rectangular and globally coupled environment classes are kept distinct;
* the desired theory is finite sample and Rényi based rather than only a first order KL asymptotic theory.

This is **not** a novelty theorem. It is the result of the present targeted literature search. Any eventual novelty statement must be audited against these neighbouring literatures at theorem statement level.

## 12. What is immediately usable

### Usable now

1. **Information clock viewpoint.** Stop insisting that information is `n` times a constant. Define model and policy dependent cumulative information first.
2. **Validity versus optimality hierarchy.** Prove error control under weaker assumptions, then add growth assumptions for stopping time and optimality.
3. **Adaptive likelihood machinery.** Adaptive log likelihood versus generalised wrong class likelihood is a plausible local composite statistic when the model admits it.
4. **Rare event importance sampling.** Calibrate small path error probabilities by a coherent change of measure towards the closest wrong path or environment.
5. **Controlled sensing literature as the policy baseline.** Any future global policy theorem must be compared against Chernoff style and dynamic programming active testing.
6. **Markov and AR examples.** Use these as controlled benchmark classes after adding explicit action dependence.

### Usable only after modification

1. Xing's global misclassification thresholds. They require a new path event argument before use.
2. The sum of the `s` smallest information functions. This relies on independent parallel streams and does not carry over to a selected path.
3. The iid composite information distance. In our problem the controlled environment may be coupled across time and histories.
4. Standard active testing control policies. In that literature, the sensing action is usually separate from the terminal hypothesis decision. Our edge action has semantic testing meaning and is irreversible in the path.

### Not usable as a direct theorem

1. Xing's asymptotic optimality result as a theorem for our model.
2. Tensorisation across stages.
3. Independent per history worst case choices in a coupled environment.
4. Any novelty claim based only on action dependent observations, composite hypotheses, temporal dependence, Markov observations, or adaptive sensing.

## 13. Research consequences

The literature search suggests that the main research question should be sharpened from

> what happens when sequential data are dependent?

to

> what finite sample information and error recursion holds when the **reported binary decision itself** selects the next composite statistical experiment, and how does this interact with coherent uncertainty across the decision tree?

The strongest near term directions are:

1. derive a one step finite sample testing inequality at a positive reach history using the exact controlled path Hellinger or Rényi quantity;
2. identify the correct controlled information clock for stopping or asymptotic questions, without assuming linear stage growth;
3. compare a decision as control model against the standard active testing model in which sensing control and terminal decision are separate;
4. construct rare event importance sampling on a coherent controlled path law;
5. use controlled finite state Markov examples as the first nontrivial model class in which both the exact `G_t` recursion and active testing asymptotics can be compared.

## 15. Reading depth and search limitations

Xing's accepted paper and supplement were read directly. The primary pages or arXiv abstracts of the controlled sensing, active testing, dependent stream, Bayesian composite, finite sample active testing, and hidden Markov Rényi papers listed above were checked. Several are obvious candidates for a subsequent full theorem level audit before we claim that a specific result is absent.

The current search found no direct theorem matching the complete `sequential_testing` formulation, but this should be recorded only as **no direct match located in this search**, not as a claim of novelty.
